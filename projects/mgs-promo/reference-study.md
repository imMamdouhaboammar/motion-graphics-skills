## Structure and rhythm

- A hard reset every 1.4 to 2 s. The world flips between warm cream paper and near-black on most cuts (light, dark, dark, light, dark, light, light, dark, light, dark). The flip itself is the rhythm.
- Density climbs in the middle (the pinned "Value" note with three icon lines is the busiest frame) and falls to one line of type at the end.
- Holds are short, but each shot settles for at least 0.5 s after its last word lands.
- The ending is the quietest frame in the film.

## Composition

- One focal object per shot, very large, often cropped by the frame (bulb, suit, keyboard, briefcase). Scale contrast between the object and the words is extreme.
- Negative space is generous. Most frames use under 40 % of the canvas.
- Placement moves: left-heavy bulb, centred suit, lower-right keyboard, centred type only. It never repeats the same layout twice in a row.

## Typography

- One grotesque sans family in two weights. Hierarchy comes from size and colour: a small dark lead-in ("You've got the", "It rewards") and a big red payoff word ("content", "strategy").
- Words arrive one at a time with a short blur-rise (about 4 frames of motion blur, no overshoot). No character-by-character typing.
- 1 to 5 visible words per frame. The narration carries the detail; the frame carries the key word.
- Type sits inside the object world: inside the round head, behind the emoji, over the keyboard.

## Palette behaviour

- Cream (#F7F3EC-ish) and near-black only, plus one red accent that marks the payoff word. The red never becomes a background.
- Brand colours of third parties (the Instagram gradient) appear once, as quoted material.

## Material and texture

- Cutout photography with soft contact shadows on cream; halftone dot treatment on the suit figure; faint grid paper; a barcode and tiny "HD / 4K" print marks as editorial furniture; tape on the keyboard.
- Soft glow on white type in dark shots.

## Motion families

1. Hard cut with a world flip (the dominant family).
2. Circular mask reset: a dot grows from a word and becomes the next scene's field (red ring at 10.0 s, black dot at 12.5 s).
3. Word-by-word blur rise for type.
4. Slow push on held objects (bulb, suit), no floating.

## What to borrow as principles

- Flip the world on the cut, not with a dissolve.
- One focal object, huge, cropped with intent; one accent word per frame.
- Word-level arrival, short blur, no bounce.
- A reset that grows out of something already in the frame.
- End on the simplest frame.

## What not to copy

- The bulb, suit, briefcase, emoji, clown, Instagram profile, "Value" note, barcode, print marks, the red accent colour, the exact shot order or any line of copy.

## Implications for this film

- Our VO is 26.1 s (reference 17.9 s) with 9 semantic beats, so shots run 1.5 to 4 s. Hold rhythm with world flips on the major beats and keep internal motion to one idea per beat.
- Our accent is a clay orange tied to the terminal cursor, not red.
- Our material is the studio desk: paper, tape, cutout tools, a real command line, the film's own script and its own waveform.
- Arabic needs word and phrase units only; our reveals are masked rises and a block cursor that cuts, not per-letter typing.
