# Reference analysis

A motion reference is evidence of a design language.

Do not reduce it to "same colors" or "same transitions."

## Video intake

Record:

- duration
- dimensions
- frame rate
- audio
- aspect ratio
- average shot length
- first-frame state
- final-frame state

## Contact sheets

Create:

1. whole-film contact sheet at a regular interval
2. first-three-seconds sheet at a denser interval
3. transition sheet around major cuts
4. optional typography sheet
5. optional palette sheet

A contact sheet shows patterns that are hard to notice during playback.

## Analyze six layers

### 1. Structure

Ask:

- how many scenes?
- where are resets?
- where does density increase?
- where does the film become quiet?
- how long are holds?
- how long is the CTA?

### 2. Composition

Ask:

- centered or asymmetric?
- large crop or contained?
- how much negative space?
- what is usually foreground?
- what is usually background?
- how often does the camera change scale?

### 3. Typography

Ask:

- serif, sans, mono, display?
- one family or multiple?
- dominant size ratios?
- word, line, or glyph animation?
- alignment?
- crop behavior?
- how much spoken copy appears?

### 4. Material

Ask:

- flat digital?
- print?
- paper?
- glass?
- photographic?
- metallic?
- soft 3D?
- hard geometric?
- hand-made?

Then inspect:

- grain
- shadows
- blur
- borders
- edge treatment
- image grade

### 5. Motion

Classify important moves:

- reveal
- transform
- handoff
- match cut
- mask
- camera
- path
- morph
- fold
- stack
- orbit
- parallax
- wipe
- occlusion

Find the dominant families.

### 6. Rhythm

Mark:

- acceleration
- pause
- surprise
- proof
- reset
- climax
- resolve

## Separate grammar from assets

Safe to learn:

- rhythm
- transition logic
- hierarchy
- scale contrast
- material category
- composition strategy
- type behavior
- camera behavior

Do not copy:

- proprietary logo
- exact copy
- exact illustration
- exact photo
- exact layout sequence
- branded character
- unique campaign mark

The target is similar craft language with original project-specific content.

## Write the reference study

Keep it concrete.

Use sections:

- technical profile
- visual grammar
- typography
- palette behavior
- material and texture
- motion families
- pacing
- what to borrow as principles
- what not to copy
- implications for this project

## Compare new work to the reference

Do not ask "does it look identical?"

Ask:

- is hierarchy as decisive?
- is type as intentional?
- are transitions as motivated?
- is the palette as controlled?
- is the frame density as disciplined?
- are objects as materially coherent?
- does the opening create a promise just as quickly?
- does the ending resolve just as cleanly?
